How the data stays current

Two different jobs on two different clocks. The expensive one runs less often.

The price re-read takes 126 seconds for the 590 sites we are allowed to read — we measured it, and it runs on a timer rather than on somebody remembering to run it. Korean clinic prices change roughly once or twice a year, so a weekly re-read is comfortably ahead of them.

Every read is kept as a snapshot for 180 days, together with what changed since the one before. After that the snapshots are deleted: long enough to show a price moving, short enough that an old archive never gets mistaken for today’s price.

Both jobs are installed as machine timers that survive a reboot, so neither one depends on anyone remembering. The sweep is deliberately scheduled after the moment the monthly search allowance refreshes — that refresh falls on the first of each month at 09:00 KST, which we measured from the API’s own rate-limit headers, so the sweep runs at 10:00 that day and then again on Thursdays. Waking up before the allowance refreshes would waste the run.

How we look for the 1,778 clinics that have no website

Rules only. No model reads a page and guesses.

1 · Look the clinic up. We ask a search API for the clinic by name, restricted to the district, with the registry’s own coordinates as the centre point.
2 · Check the address. We take the registry’s coordinates and the search result’s coordinates and compare. If they are within 40 metres, it is the same building.
3 · Check the name or the telephone number. One of the two has to match as well. Both filters have to pass before we accept a URL.
4 · Read the site for prices. Only once a URL is accepted do we open the site and look for a treatment with an amount.
5 · Remember it. An accepted URL is written to a registry file and never searched for again. A clinic that failed is put on a backoff — one month, then three, then six, then it stops.
6 · Skip the hosts we do not collect from. 21 sites — 13 that state plainly that everything is disallowed, 8 whose robots.txt we cannot read — are never accepted, even when a search points straight at them.
measured We ran the full pipeline on 36 clinics in two runs. 35 of the 36 produced a URL candidate, 16 passed the address and name checks, and 1 of those confirmed sites had a treatment price we could read. That last number is the honest one: finding the website does not mean the clinic publishes a price. The second run stopped after 6 clinics because the monthly search allowance ran out, so the sample is small.
free allowance One or two requests per clinic. When the coordinate search already produces a match we stop there and skip the wider district search, so 1,778 clinics take roughly 1,800–3,500 requests rather than twice that many. We do not buy those requests: the free monthly allowance covers 750 of them a month, and every answer is cached, so a clinic is never searched twice.
projection Across the whole pool, 44 of 2,387 clinics publish a price we can read — 1.8%. Applied to the 1,778, that is roughly 33 more clinics with a readable price. The sweep also produces a correct website for about 790 of them, which is worth having even where no price follows.
!
Free allowance only, spread over about four months

Our search plan includes $5 of free requests a month — 1,000 requests. We give the sweep 750 of them and keep the rest for our own searching, and we do not buy more. At the measured rate of one to two requests per clinic that is about 480 clinics a month, so all 1,778 take about four months. The sweep does not need to be repeated — every answer it finds is cached — so the cost of waiting is only that the list grows more slowly.

We measured when the allowance refreshes rather than guessing: the API’s own rate-limit headers put the reset at 09:00 KST on the first of the month, two seconds off the month boundary. The sweep is scheduled an hour after that — the 1st at 10:00 KST, then every Thursday at 06:20 KST — so a run can never wake up before the allowance exists.

So the sweep is built to run itself within that allowance. It counts its own requests, stops at the cap without changing anything else, and picks up again on the next scheduled run. We also read the remaining balance the server reports rather than trusting the number on disk, because the allowance is shared with other tools of ours that also search the web: our own count said two requests, the account said a thousand spent.

And it works through the districts in a fixed order — the ones where clinics actually publish prices first — so the part of the sweep most likely to find a price happens earliest.

Why parking and Saturday hours are thin

Only 284 of the 2,387 clinics have a detailed registry record at all — the file that carries parking spaces and Saturday times. The other 2,103 have a name, an address and an opening date, and nothing more. We checked the detailed-record API as well: of the 978 records it returns, every clinic that carries any of these fields is already inside that 284. It adds no coverage we did not have.

So the two thin filters are a gap in the source, not in our collection. We still offer them, and we show “not in the registry” as its own option rather than leaving you to wonder whether a missing value means no parking or no record.

We audit the rules on every site before we collect from it.

We never visit the same host twice within a second, and we wait exactly as long as a site asks for when it sets a crawl-delay.

What a price on this site is

The definition we hold ourselves to.

own page The figure is on the clinic’s own website, in the clinic’s own wording. We do not estimate, convert, average or adjust it.
promotion The clinic itself labels the line as a first-visit or event price. We keep it and mark it, and we leave it out when we take a clinic’s middle figure.
package The figure covers more than one treatment — a device plus a filler, or a course of sessions. That is not a price for one treatment, so it does not enter any range on this site.
not published The clinic is in the registry but publishes no amount we can read. It gets no price on this site, and no guess.

Units are not normalised. A clinic that lists ₩50,000 per 100U and a clinic that lists ₩500,000 for a full face are both correct at what they publish — we show both and label the unit.

What we cannot tell you

The list is longer than the page. This is the honest version of it.

  • Quality. We have not read a single patient review. Nothing on this site ranks a clinic by how good it is.
  • Price for you. The unit, the area and the amount are decided at consultation. A published figure is a starting point, not a quote.
  • Which clinic is good value. We can tell you what a clinic charges. We cannot tell you whether the result will be worth it, and we do not score clinics.
  • Every clinic. 2,343 of the 2,387 clinics in the three districts are not listed. They publish no price we can read. See what we do list →
  • Real-time prices. We re-read weekly. Between reads a clinic can change its list, and we would not know until the next read.
  • A finished directory. 44 is what is listed as of 24 October 2026, not a final number. The search sweep that fills the remaining 1,778 is running in slices, so the list grows and the price coverage on this page moves with it.
  • Doctor, hours, language, reception. The registry does not hold these. We do not print them for a clinic we have not verified, so for most clinics on this site they are blank.
  • Whether a price is the final one. Prices may be quoted before VAT, and packages work out at a different effective rate. The clinic confirms at consultation.

All of Seoul

5,444 skin and plastic clinics across the 25 districts, and 1,103 of them (20.3%) already have a website on file. In our three districts, 44 of the 609 clinics that had one published a price we could read — 7.2%. Applied to Seoul’s 1,103, that is about 80 clinics. The search sweep would find more websites across the city, and the sites it finds publish prices less often, so this is a conservative figure.
